Data processing terms

Last updated October 2026. These terms form part of your Speed to Lead subscription.

Our assistant answers calls from your customers on your behalf. For those calls you are the controller of your customers' details and we are your processor. These terms set out how we look after that information, as UK data protection law (UK GDPR, Article 28) requires.

1. What we process and why

Callers' names, phone numbers, postcodes, the reason for their call, booking details, call recordings and transcripts. We use them only to answer, book and pass on calls for you, and to show you call summaries and figures.

2. Your instructions

We only process this information on your documented instructions: the call script and settings you approve. We tell you straight away if we think an instruction breaks the law.

3. Confidentiality and security

Everyone who handles the information is bound by confidentiality. We use appropriate security, including encrypted connections and access limited to the people who need it.

4. Sub-processors

We use trusted suppliers for telephony, voice and AI transcription, and hosting. They are bound by the same obligations as us. We will tell you before adding or replacing one, and you may object.

5. Helping you

We help you respond to requests from callers (for example to see or delete their information), and with any data protection impact assessment or contact with the Information Commissioner's Office.

6. Breaches

If personal information is lost or accessed without permission, we tell you without undue delay, and within 48 hours of finding out, with the details you need.

7. How long we keep it

Call recordings are kept for the period you chose when you signed up, then deleted. When your subscription ends we delete or return the information, unless the law requires us to keep it.

8. Transfers

Where a supplier processes information outside the UK, we rely on UK adequacy regulations or the International Data Transfer Agreement.

9. Audits

We give you the information you reasonably need to show these terms are being met, and allow reasonable audits on notice.
