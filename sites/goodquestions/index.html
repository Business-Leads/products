Taking part is not an enquiry

Answering fifteen questions about your business is not a request to be sold to, and it is not treated as one. At the end you are asked, separately, whether you would like help improving your score. Say no and you keep your report and hear nothing further.

Questions people ask

How long does the assessment take?

Fifteen questions and about three minutes. Your score appears on screen as soon as you finish, and the written version follows by email.

What happens to my answers?

They produce your own score and report, and they are combined with everybody else's to show how UK businesses are doing as a whole. Published findings are reported as totals and percentages, never as named businesses.

Will somebody ring me afterwards?

Only if you ask. The last question is whether you would like help improving your score. Say no and you keep your report and hear nothing further.

Is this useful if we have barely started with AI?

That is often when it is most useful. A low score is not a verdict, it is a list of the three or four things worth doing first, in an order that makes sense.

Can our organisation sponsor an assessment?

Yes. We design the questions with you, put them to UK business decision-makers, and write up what comes back. Each campaign is quoted on the audience, the research and the reporting involved, so the sensible first step is a conversation.

What are the assessments built on?

The questions, the scoring, the audience, the research and the reporting are all ours.
